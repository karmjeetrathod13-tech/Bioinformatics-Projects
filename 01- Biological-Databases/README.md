# Experiment 1: Exploring Biological Databases

## Objective

To explore and use different biological databases for retrieving biological information relevant to bioinformatics research.

## Gene of Interest

- **Gene:** LYZ
- **Protein:** Lysozyme C
- **Organism:** Homo sapiens

## Introduction

Biological databases are organized repositories that store biological information such as nucleotide sequences, protein sequences, protein structures, functional annotations, and biological pathways.

In this experiment, different biological databases were explored using lysozyme as the biological topic of interest.

## Databases Explored

| Database | Information Explored |
|----------|----------------------|
| NCBI GenBank | Nucleotide sequence and gene information |
| UniProt | Protein sequence and functional information |
| KEGG | Biological pathway information |
| PDB | Three-dimensional protein structures |

## 1. NCBI GenBank

The NCBI Nucleotide database was searched for human lysozyme.

### Observed Information

- **Description:** Human lysozyme mRNA, complete cds
- **Accession:** M19045.1
- **Sequence length:** 1,483 bp
- **Organism:** Homo sapiens
- **Molecule:** mRNA

The nucleotide sequence and GenBank record were examined.

## 2. UniProt

UniProt was explored to obtain protein-level information for human lysozyme.

### Observed Information

- **UniProt ID:** P61626
- **Entry Name:** LYSC_HUMAN
- **Protein:** Lysozyme C
- **Gene:** LYZ
- **Organism:** Homo sapiens
- **Protein length:** 148 amino acids
- **Status:** UniProtKB reviewed (Swiss-Prot)
- **Protein existence:** Evidence at protein level

The protein sequence and functional annotation were also examined.

## 3. KEGG

The KEGG database was explored to study biological pathway information.

The following pathway was observed:

- **Pathway ID:** map00010
- **Pathway:** Glycolysis / Gluconeogenesis
- **Class:** Metabolism; Carbohydrate metabolism

The pathway map was examined to understand the organization of metabolic reactions.

## 4. Protein Data Bank (PDB)

The Protein Data Bank was explored to examine available three-dimensional protein structures related to lysozyme.

### Observed Structure

- **PDB ID:** 168L
- **Title:** Protein flexibility and adaptability seen in 25 crystal forms of T4 lysozyme
- **Organism:** Tequatrovirus T4
- **Macromolecule:** T4 lysozyme
- **Experimental method:** X-ray diffraction
- **Resolution:** 2.90 Å

> **Note:** PDB entry 168L is a T4 lysozyme structure and is not the human LYZ protein structure. It is therefore recorded as a lysozyme-related PDB observation.

## Methodology

1. Selected LYZ (Lysozyme C) as the gene/protein of interest.
2. Searched the NCBI Nucleotide database for human lysozyme.
3. Examined the corresponding GenBank record and nucleotide sequence.
4. Searched UniProt for human Lysozyme C.
5. Examined protein identification, sequence, and functional information.
6. Explored KEGG pathway information.
7. Searched PDB for available lysozyme-related structures.
8. Recorded the observed database information and screenshots.

## Results

The four biological databases were successfully explored.

The experiment demonstrated that different databases provide different types of biological information, including nucleotide sequences, protein information, metabolic pathways, and three-dimensional structures.

## Conclusion

The experiment provided practical experience in accessing and exploring biological databases. NCBI was used for nucleotide information, UniProt for protein information, KEGG for pathway information, and PDB for structural information.
